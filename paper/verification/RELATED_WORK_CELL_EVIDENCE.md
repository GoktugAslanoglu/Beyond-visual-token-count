# Primary-source verification for the positioning table

The six-row main-paper table summarizes positive results or design features of the closest predecessors and states the distinct measurement in this study. It is not a binary novelty checklist and does not rank incompatible performance numbers. Additional cited work is documented below. Full texts were inspected; downloaded literature is retained locally for verification and is not redistributed.

| Work | Primary source | Evidence for learning, representation and evaluation cells |
|---|---|---|
| Text or Pixels? | [EMNLP 2025 Findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.558/) | Introduction explicitly states no fine-tuning/supervision. Methods render text with off-the-shelf readers; experiments use RULER and CNN/DailyMail and report inference timing. The numeric single-needle study uses GPT-4.1-mini and Qwen2.5-VL-72B, shorter contexts and an adaptive-font LaTeX renderer. This six-task reference-8k protocol cannot identify the cause of cross-paper gaps. |
| Fico | [ACL 2026 Findings](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1758/) | Introduction and benchmark construction describe seven density/fidelity variants, fixed evaluation of general/OCR readers, and OCR/NIAH/VQA. Section 3 includes binarization, JPEG and upscaling perturbations that preserve the reported compression ratio. Equal-ratio utility variation is prior art; this manuscript adds stricter paired native-allocation controls. |
| DeepSeek-OCR | [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18234) | Sections 3.2–3.5 specify encoder/decoder and training. Section 4.1 evaluates vision-text compression and reconstruction. No inference that OCR proves downstream utility. |
| Glyph | [ACL 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1722/) | Methods specify continual pretraining, rendering search, SFT/RL and speed measurements. Table 5 compares rendering configurations at similar ratios. Section 4.3 identifies rare order-sensitive strings as difficult. |
| VIST | [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00791) | Section 3: frozen vision encoder, trained Perceiver resampler and cross-attention interface, distant visual plus proximal text input. Experiments evaluate downstream tasks and computational resources. “Resampler, interface” does not imply every base weight is updated. |
| LensVLM | [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07019) | Sections 3.2–3.5 and Figure 2 describe expansion. Section 8 includes conditional evidence-hit analysis; Section 9 evaluates expansion without additional training. Neither fixed readers nor a coverage/fidelity distinction is exclusive to this study. |
| AgentOCR | [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04786) | Agentic RL formulation and method describe cached optical history and learned self-compression; evaluation measures agent success and token cost. |
| MemOCR | [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21468) | Introduction and method describe persistent rich-text memory, adaptive visual salience, and budget-aware RL; QA experiments study memory budgets. No claim that this paper originated layout-aware memory. |
| OCR-Memory | [ACL 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.474/) | Retrieval adaptation section and implementation details: visual encoder frozen, language decoder fine-tuned for anchor selection. Original text is fetched deterministically; Mind2Web/AppWorld evaluate long-horizon agents. The table correctly marks retrieval-decoder adaptation rather than an entirely fixed pipeline. |
| VizoMem | [ACL 2026 Findings](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.365/) | Introduction, ColGlyph training and evaluation: a trained visual-note retriever, query-conditioned retrieval, and LoCoMo/LongMemEval recall. |
| Lee et al. | [April 2026 revision, v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.03643v2) | Sections 2–4 compare optical, pooling and hierarchical encoders with controlled decoder/training conditions. Evaluations distinguish reconstruction, language modeling and factual recall; recall uses answer log-likelihood, unlike the generated-answer scores here. Optical recall can beat truncation while direct alternatives remain stronger. This makes the critique directly relevant without contradicting the narrower GLM/hash-retention result. |
| AGAR | [Version-pinned primary paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12898v1) | Sections 3.3-3.4 study localization, recovery and evidence magnification. Section 5.1 defines the token-matched Uniform control; Tables 2-3 report close aggregate token counts. The manuscript distinguishes identical per-case native counts and IDs, without implying that prior work lacked token-budget controls. |
| This study | Authoritative analyses and frozen protocols in the evidence manifest | Fixed evaluated readers; paired native-rate geometry interventions; query-agnostic hash ranking and target-survival accounting; exact identifier retrieval, density sweep, dialogue controls and systems profiles. These are diagnostic interventions, not an added modular architecture. |

## Added bibliography checks

Text or Pixels? metadata was retrieved from the publisher's BibTeX: Yanhong Li, Zixuan Lan and Jiawei Zhou; Findings of EMNLP 2025; pages 10564–10578; DOI 10.18653/v1/2025.findings-emnlp.558.

The autoencoding critique cites the April 2026 v2 specifically. Its initial posting was December 2025; the entry explicitly records both to avoid mistaking the revision year for the initial publication year. Authors are Ivan Yee Lee, Cheng Yang and Taylor Berg-Kirkpatrick. The full v2 paper adds the factual-recall distinction used in this manuscript.

[Cover and Thomas, Elements of Information Theory, second edition](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/book/10.1002/047174882X) is verified against the publisher record (copyright 2006, DOI 10.1002/047174882X); Chapter 10 is rate–distortion theory. The revised paper uses rate–distortion vocabulary as background but no longer displays the reconstruction-channel infimum. Its measured native visual-token rate is not Shannon mutual information or bits.


The gatekeeper revision also cites Measure Transport (https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06708v1) for task-relevant loss and cross-patch fragmentation, and ZeroSense (https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01848v1) for full-text recovery under reduced contextual predictability. Neither is described as implementing this paper's source-record inclusion diagnostic.
